01

Vision

Enterprises will run agents as colleagues. The ones that scale will be the ones whose agents can disagree safely: the dissent is kept, evidence decides, and a person is asked only when it matters.

What I believeWhat it implies for the product
Agents are becoming first-class workers with identities and permissions (Entra Agent ID, Workday Agent System of Record)Conflicts between agents become an operating problem, not a research curiosity
The scarce resource is the person with authority, not computeSell throughput: more decisions act safely without them
Convergence should be a state update, not a longer conversationRead the evidence behind an action; never add another debate loop
Reliability is earned in a loop, not shipped in a releaseIntervene at inference, evaluate in shadow, prove improvement over time

The direction I am committing to, before the solution

A closed reliability loop for multi-agent workflows, at three time scales, built from today's agents and LLMs:

LoopWhenWhat it doesParallel in Dyssonance's thesis
1. InterveneAt inferenceBefore a high-impact write, check the evidence: contested by another agent or a newer source, or stale. Return act, gather or holdWhether the system "knows enough to act"; dissonance and decay
2. LearnDuring and after the runTrace every run; judges score in shadow without acting; offline replay of traces and planted defects; human rulings become eval casesBelief revision as evidence arrives
3. ProveOver weeksOnline evals on live runs, benchmarked against the past; regressions trip the kill switchTransitions between states as learning signals
Why build the harness-era version on purpose

Loops 1 to 3 are the best version possible with today's tools. The deliverable is the evidence: what the loop costs, where it still fails, and a replacement map of which parts a persistent belief state would subsume, improve, or leave untouched. It need not be their architecture to test their hypothesis.

02

The failure mode

What breaks, stated as an observable behaviour, with no solution in the sentence.

An agent commits an action on a belief that is contested or stale, and nothing records what it relied on. The most visible and fastest-growing source of contest is other agents: when two specialists reach different conclusions about the same object, today's systems settle it by mechanics (last write wins, the most confident agent acts, or the coordinator picks), and the action executes against the system of record.

Evidence: Spawn, an agent-native company landing about 108 changes an hour, resolves agent conflicts by letting whoever lands second reconcile, and keeps no record of the competing positions (Spawn CEO blog, 28 Oct 2026, pp. 3 to 6). Academic failure taxonomies find inter-agent misalignment is a large share of multi-agent failures, though on research coding and maths tasks (MAST, arXiv 2503.13657). The enterprise rate is unmeasured; that is the first thing to find out.

How I chose

03

The business problem

The same failure in the language of the person paying for it.

Operations owners see agent actions reversed, refunds clawed back, customers told the wrong status, and a growing queue of escalations they cannot clear.

Each one costs handle time and rework, and in regulated workflows a wrong irreversible action becomes a loss or a finding. The per-incident cost is unmeasured assumed material; the log pull in section 06 replaces this line with a number.

Enterprise structure makes it harder: RBAC means the agent that can act is often not the one that knows; hierarchy means some views outrank others only while current; PII rules limit what an escalation may show to whom.

Why now

Organisations orchestrating multiple agents rose from 9% to 18% in one quarter (KPMG Q2 2026 AI Pulse: 204 US leaders at firms above $1B revenue). Gartner predicted in June 2025 that over 40% of agentic AI projects will be cancelled by end 2027, citing cost, unclear value or inadequate risk controls. Agents now get directory identities and HR records (Entra Agent ID; Workday Agent System of Record). Spawn and Block show where action volume is heading; they are colour, not proof of enterprise pain.

From one practitioner conversation, 2 Nov: one view, not evidence

The objection I expected was wrong. Finance often knows the rough size of this leak, because quarter-end reconciliation forces it. What it cannot say is which agent decision caused it, so the pitch is attribution, not "you have a hidden problem". Why not ask the vendor: the conflict cuts across systems, and a single vendor has little reason to prioritise that. How to enter: a free seven-day parallel run, paid only if what it finds is worth more than it costs. Finance checks it against numbers it already trusts.

Partly a trend chase, and I am treating it that way. Spawn is ten people writing code, and Block's restructuring is about one agent, not agents disagreeing. They are used as colour for where action volume goes, never as evidence. The evidence has to come from five operations-owner interviews and one log pull counting reversals and overrides, before design is committed.

Constraints carried into design

Converge by updating state, not by longer agent debate, which is the token maximalism C. Lozano argues against ("The Internet of Agents: Google's Surface War, Token Maximalism, and the Benchmark That Pretended to Surprise Us", 23 May 2026). Bounded output (act, gather, hold) from a small classifier, costed per resolved conflict, not per token. Escalate only real disagreements: users approve about 93% of Claude Code permission prompts (Anthropic, as cited by C. Lozano). Hold is a first-class output and is bias-tested with counterfactual pairs, because KillBench found structured outputs collapse refusals and amplify bias (White Circle). Escalations are reviewable artifacts in the surface the person already uses, ruled on by comment, the pattern Antigravity uses to supervise parallel agents; Antigravity's artifacts are per agent, and nothing adjudicates across agents touching the same object. Evaluate on planted defects in our own workflow, not a leaderboard.

Discovery callout · decision, trade-off, conclusion · fit with the belief-state thesis

Decision. Build the strongest harness-era version on purpose (intervene, learn, prove) as the baseline, not the product.

Trade-off. It risks reading as more guardrails, the symptom containment Lozano argues against. In return it produces evidence of where harnesses break, which nobody has published.

Conclusion. The idea supports the thesis only if three things hold: the loops are framed as the baseline; what they learn is stored as revisable state with provenance (source reliability, how fast each fact goes stale, overturned rulings), not as prompt edits; and the demo shows one case the harness cannot solve.

Belief is bias. A belief is a prior held with confidence; unaudited, it is bias. Every learned belief carries provenance and is tested with counterfactual pairs, the KillBench lesson applied to state rather than output.

Decision. What the loop learns is stored as a standing rule with an owner and an expiry, never as a prompt edit.

Where the harness hits its ceiling: v4

v3 had two cases it could not express. The Chargebee lag: no freshness window can say "the credit list lags writes by over a minute". CT-08: the model flips between runs, and the harness throws that disagreement away. Underneath both, autonomy rested on model silence. So v4 ran the test this section promised, in small form: hard vetoes stay, and every soft judgement moves to a belief layer of a few pure functions, with no new dependencies.

  • Age, not a cliff. An observation's weight stays flat while fresh, is 0.5 at the old window, and falls after it.
  • Silence must be earned. Each model check cites a record ID, and code confirms it exists in what was sent. A sample that cannot cite a contract document does not count as clean. This shows the record was cited, not that the model read it.
  • Disagreement is evidence. When the answer can change the decision, the model runs three times, and the share raising a conflict maps to P(safe): 0.70, 0.30 or 0.05.
  • An expected-value decision. Act if (1 - P(safe)) x stake is below the cost of delay; otherwise gather the fact whose re-read cuts expected loss most; hold if no re-read can help. P(safe) is a product over facts and assumes independence.
  • Rulings are evidence. Keeping a hold lowers the reliability of the source in doubt; committing raises it.

Priors, all assumed and fixed before the first full run: source reliability 0.995 (contracts 0.99); cost of delay $20, of which $2.67 is measured review time at an assumed rate; credit list τ 90 s, from one lag seen on the live Chargebee test site. An unread fact is 0.8, which is a pessimistic prior set by policy, "do not act on unknowns", not a measured base rate. Real base rates are probably much higher, and a log pull would give them. These values mean nothing above about $800 acts, and below about $94 the contract is not read.

Open questions before anyone builds this

  1. Do conflicts reach costly volume outside agent-native firms, or is staleness the whole problem? Five interviews and one log pull answer this.
  2. Will a real agent framework let money actions be intercepted before commit, across two or more vendors?
  3. What miss rate would finance accept for autonomous credits, and what over-hold rate would support accept?
  4. Who is the daily user the product wins or loses on: the engineer who owns the boundary, or the analyst who rules?
  5. Does a full stateful engine beat this small layer once evidence arrives as a stream, not one case at a time?
Where this plan is weak

1. No customer has confirmed the problem. The evidence is public opinion classified by a model. 2. The results grade themselves. The same model wrote the system and its tests, on synthetic data and a small sample. 3. The live path is real but small. A real agent, real test systems and real clicks, on scripted tickets, seeded accounts and one kind of write. It proves the path works, not that a buyer needs it.
