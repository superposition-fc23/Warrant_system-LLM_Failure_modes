01

Vision

Enterprises will run agents as colleagues. The ones that scale will be the ones whose agents can disagree safely: the dissent is kept, evidence decides, and a person is asked only when it matters.

What I believeWhat it implies for the product
Agents are becoming first-class workers with identities and permissions (Entra Agent ID, Workday Agent System of Record)Conflicts between agents become an operating problem, not a research curiosity
The scarce resource is the person with authority, not computeSell throughput: more decisions act safely without them
Convergence should be a state update, not a longer conversationRead the evidence behind an action; never add another debate loop
Reliability is earned in a loop, not shipped in a releaseIntervene at inference, evaluate in shadow, prove improvement over time

The direction I am committing to, before the solution

A closed reliability loop for multi-agent workflows, at three time scales, built from today's agents and LLMs:

LoopWhenWhat it doesParallel in Dyssonance's thesis
1. InterveneAt inferenceBefore a high-impact write, check the evidence: contested by another agent or a newer source, or stale. Return act, gather or holdWhether the system "knows enough to act"; dissonance and decay
2. LearnDuring and after the runTrace every run; judges score in shadow without acting; offline replay of traces and planted defects; human rulings become eval casesBelief revision as evidence arrives
3. ProveOver weeksOnline evals on live runs, benchmarked against the past; regressions trip the kill switchTransitions between states as learning signals
Why build the harness-era version on purpose

Loops 1 to 3 are the best version possible with today's tools. The deliverable is the evidence: what the loop costs, where it still fails, and a replacement map of which parts a persistent belief state would subsume, improve, or leave untouched. It need not be their architecture to test their hypothesis.

02

The failure mode

What breaks, stated as an observable behaviour, with no solution in the sentence.

An agent commits an action on a belief that is contested or stale, and nothing records what it relied on. The most visible and fastest-growing source of contest is other agents: when two specialists reach different conclusions about the same object, today's systems settle it by mechanics (last write wins, the most confident agent acts, or the coordinator picks), and the action executes against the system of record.

Evidence: Spawn, an agent-native company landing about 108 changes an hour, resolves agent conflicts by letting whoever lands second reconcile, and keeps no record of the competing positions (Spawn CEO blog, 28 Oct 2026, pp. 3 to 6). Academic failure taxonomies find inter-agent misalignment is a large share of multi-agent failures, though on research coding and maths tasks (MAST, arXiv 2503.13657). The enterprise rate is unmeasured; that is the first thing to find out.

How I chose

Frequent enough to matter, expensive when it happens, invisible to the person it hurts until too late, and containable by a product rather than only by a better model. A better model in each agent still disagrees with a better model in the next one; conflicting evidence is not a capability gap.

03

The business problem

The same failure in the language of the person paying for it.

Operations owners see agent actions reversed, refunds clawed back, customers told the wrong status, and a growing queue of escalations they cannot clear.

Each one costs handle time and rework, and in regulated workflows a wrong irreversible action becomes a loss or a finding. The per-incident cost is unmeasured assumed material; the log pull in section 06 replaces this line with a number.

VerticalThe conflictThe rule that existsWhere the rule fails
Retail bankingService agent changes the phone number and enrols a new device; minutes later the payments agent sends an instant payment to a new payee (Zelle or RTP in the US, SEPA Instant or Faster Payments in Europe) on a device-risk score that predates the changeAccount-takeover rule holds new payees after a contact or device changeBlocks every genuine customer with a new phone; cannot see they passed step-up in the app an hour ago, held in another system
FreightException agent tells the customer a container is released while the broker shows an exam holdCustoms status outranks carrierCannot express that the hold is six hours old, or the exam finished but was not posted
CRM serviceRefund agent approves a credit while risk has flagged the account and the contract overrides termsRisk outranks serviceCannot tell the flag was cleared yesterday or the contract was amended

Enterprise structure makes it harder: RBAC means the agent that can act is often not the one that knows; hierarchy means some views outrank others only while current; PII rules limit what an escalation may show to whom.

Why now

Organisations orchestrating multiple agents rose from 9% to 18% in one quarter (KPMG AI Pulse, to verify). Gartner expects over 40% of agentic AI projects cancelled by end 2027, partly over risk controls. Agents now get directory identities and HR records (Entra Agent ID; Workday Agent System of Record). Spawn and Block show where action volume is heading; they are colour, not proof of enterprise pain.

Is the Spawn and Block direction a judgement call or a trend chase?

Partly a trend chase, and I am treating it that way. Spawn is ten people writing code, and Block's restructuring is about one agent, not agents disagreeing. They are used as colour for where action volume goes, never as evidence. The evidence has to come from five operations-owner interviews and one log pull counting reversals and overrides, before design is committed.

Constraints carried into design

Converge by updating state, not by longer agent debate, which is the token maximalism C. Lozano argues against ("The Internet of Agents: Google's Surface War, Token Maximalism, and the Benchmark That Pretended to Surprise Us", 23 May 2026). Bounded output (act, gather, hold) from a small classifier, costed per resolved conflict, not per token. Escalate only real disagreements: users approve about 93% of Claude Code permission prompts (Anthropic, as cited by C. Lozano). Hold is a first-class output and is bias-tested with counterfactual pairs, because KillBench found structured outputs collapse refusals and amplify bias (White Circle). Escalations are reviewable artifacts in the surface the person already uses, ruled on by comment, the pattern Antigravity uses to supervise parallel agents; Antigravity's artifacts are per agent, and nothing adjudicates across agents touching the same object. Evaluate on planted defects in our own workflow, not a leaderboard.

Discovery callout · decision, trade-off, conclusion · fit with the belief-state thesis

Decision. Build the strongest harness-era version on purpose (intervene, learn, prove) as the baseline, not the product.

Trade-off. It risks reading as more guardrails, the symptom containment Lozano argues against. In return it produces evidence of where harnesses break, which nobody has published.

Conclusion. The idea supports the thesis only if three things hold: the loops are framed as the baseline; what they learn is stored as revisable state with provenance (source reliability, how fast each fact goes stale, overturned rulings), not as prompt edits; and the demo shows one case the harness cannot solve.

Belief is bias. A belief is a prior held with confidence; unaudited, it is bias. Every learned belief carries provenance and is tested with counterfactual pairs, the KillBench lesson applied to state rather than output.

This evidence is itself a failure mode, so it is labelled as one

Selection: authors are developers and commentators; 1 of 181 read as finance, 10 as executives, 90 could not be placed. Query bias: I wrote the search terms from my hypothesis. Judge bias: a model inferred every score, and its scores compress toward the middle (all opportunities fall between 6.8 and 9.7); belief is bias, and so is the judge. Caught in the build: the first report averaged the top 100 by importance, which inflated importance by 0.6 to 1.0 per outcome; it now uses every relevant item. Caught again on the rerun: Algolia matches every word, so 7 of 10 long severity queries returned nothing; that was a recall failure, not evidence, and they were shortened before the rule was applied. Not our customers: nothing shows these authors run multi-agent support. Use it to rank and to falsify, not to size. Interviews still decide.

What the re-estimate changed

1. Shadow mode (R9) moved from a feature to the entry wedge. It answers the objection that a customer will not let a new tool touch a money flow: in shadow it touches nothing and still prices what it would have stopped. That removes most of the case for starting with severity instead. 2. The riskiest assumption is not the gate, it is R1's inputs: that conflicting evidence is frequent and readable from tool calls and timestamped records. So P1 is shadow mode with R1, R2 and R5 on historical tickets, no blocking. 3. R6 ships thin. Exact-pattern rules prove that learning is stored as state; generalising rules waits for real rulings to learn from. 4. R8 is a sales requirement, not a pilot one, so the pilot redacts and the full version follows the first paying customer.

The decision this changes: today the engineer decides once, for all money actions, whether agents may act alone, in a prompt or a permission toggle, and never revisits it with evidence. With this, the decision is made per action type, from shadow evidence, and widened or narrowed week by week.

Goals and non-goals

Goal. Let support agents take money actions alone only when the evidence across systems agrees and is current, so the VP of Support can widen automation without raising reversals, and prove it week on week.

Money action: an agent action that writes a value someone owes or is owed, such as a credit, refund, discount, entitlement grant, plan change, write-off or SLA credit. Money consequence: a case outcome that costs money without a ledger write, such as a severity downgrade that ends in an SLA breach, or churn. G1 gates money actions; money consequences are measured in G4.

Non-goals for v1

  • Not replacing the CRM vendor's agents or orchestrator, because it is not the same job: their agents resolve the ticket; this decides whether the evidence across billing, contract and risk, systems the CRM does not own, is good enough to commit, independently of the vendor whose agent is being checked. It is separation of duties. It ships inside the CRM as a marketplace app, so the user sees one tool. It targets customers with two or more agent vendors or systems of record outside the CRM; a single-vendor shop is where the incumbent bundles this and we lose.
  • Not a tracing or observability product, because generic tracing is served; it consumes existing traces and writes only the decision record.
  • Not changing models, weights or prompts, because what the loop learns is stored as revisable state with provenance, which is the line between this and a harness.

Deferred to v2, on evidence: customer-facing contradiction checks (O4; top band in both runs, so first in line) and classification and severity disputes (P2, P3). P3 failed the rule set before the rerun (4 opinions against a bar of 10), but it stays the candidate first pilot, because a severity gate never writes to a ledger and so is easier for a buyer to say yes to. The interviews decide between that pilot and money actions. The v1 data model must already support both.

Assumptions I am making, correct me

1. Money actions leave the agents as tool calls we can see before they commit (MCP or API layer).   2. Billing, contract and risk systems expose read access with timestamps.   3. The workflow owner can name who has authority for each action type and amount.   4. Security will allow shadow-mode read access to those systems for a pilot.

Design callout · decision, trade-off, conclusion · fit with the belief-state thesis

Decision. What the loop learns is stored as a standing rule with an owner and an expiry, never as a prompt edit.

Trade-off. A rule is crude: it matches on a pattern and cannot weigh evidence. In return it can be read, attributed, expired and diffed.

Conclusion. Rules are the seed of a belief store, but they are not one. They hold policy, not how far to trust a source or how fast a fact goes stale. Section 14 maps what a belief state would replace.

Warrant is a gate between support agents and the systems that move money. When an agent proposes a credit, code reads CRM, billing, contracts and risk through a read-only MCP server, applies deterministic checks and standing rules, and asks one model call only what code cannot read: contract limits and conflicts. The test I would run. Give a persistent belief store and this harness the same cases plus a stream of evidence updates. Compare missed defects, over-holds, cost per decision and how well each explains itself after the fact. If the harness matches on all four, that is a finding too: the problem is engineering, not architecture.

Open questions before anyone builds this

  1. Do conflicts reach costly volume outside agent-native firms, or is staleness the whole problem? Five interviews and one log pull answer this.
  2. Will a real agent framework let money actions be intercepted before commit, across two or more vendors?
  3. What miss rate would finance accept for autonomous credits, and what over-hold rate would support accept?
  4. Who is the daily user the product wins or loses on: the engineer who owns the boundary, or the analyst who rules?
  5. Does a belief store beat a well-run harness on the test above?
Where this plan is weak

1. No customer has confirmed the problem. The evidence is public opinion classified by a model. 2. The results grade themselves. The same model wrote the system and its tests, on synthetic data and a small sample. 3. The gate stands in front of nothing yet. No live agent, no person receiving a question, no proof for the buyer. It demonstrates a mechanism, not a product.

Conclusions

  1. What I would cut with half the time, and what it would cost: the boundary screen, the shadow report and the third-party evidence. I would keep the live agent, one live billing system and the Slack card, because that path found the failure no fixture could. It would cost the buyer story and the engineer's view, so the demo would prove a gate, not a product.
  2. What I would want to know before anyone builds this: how often agents act on conflicting sources at firms that are not agent-native, and whether finance can tie that to the leakage it already sees at reconciliation. Five interviews and one log pull answer this. I have done neither, and it is the weakest part of this case.
  3. How I would know in 90 days whether it worked, including the counter-metric: a design partner ran shadow mode on one action type, finance confirmed a meaningful share of held money as real leakage, and the engineer widened autonomy on that evidence. Counter-metric: clean actions held stay at or below 10% (assumed limit, to be set with the partner). If they do not, the kill switch trips and nothing widens.
