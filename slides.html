02 · The failure mode

Agents act on beliefs nobody checked

An agent commits an action on a belief that is contested or stale, and nothing records what it relied on.

  • Illustration: a retention agent credits an account that risk flagged an hour ago; a second agent then credits the same incident
  • Conflicts settle by mechanics: last write wins, or the most confident agent acts
  • Spawn lets whoever lands second reconcile, and keeps no record of the competing positions
  • The enterprise rate is unmeasured. That is the first thing to find out
Sources: Spawn CEO blog, 28 Oct 2026; MAST, arXiv 2503.13657 (research tasks, not enterprise)
1:00 to 2:00. Why this failure over the others: frequent, expensive, invisible until late, and a product can contain it. A better model does not fix it, because the evidence itself disagrees.

09c · The belief layer

"Isn't this just a guardrail?"

A guardrail says yes or no. This also says how sure it is, and what would settle it.

  • Age, not a cliff: evidence fades as it gets old
  • Silence must be earned: each check cites a record, and code confirms it exists
  • Disagreement is evidence: three samples when the answer can change the decision
  • Expected loss against cost of delay: act, re-read or hold. Hard vetoes still win
P(safe) assumes independent facts. All priors assumed. The unread-fact 0.8 is a policy, not a measurement
8:00 to 9:00. A few pure functions, no new dependencies. The model stays an extractor, because its outputs are not calibrated probabilities. A cited ID shows the record exists, not that the model read it. Say this limit yourself.

11a · Where the AI was wrong

Every eval passed. Then the live agent double-credited a customer

Chargebee's credit list lagged its own write by over a minute. An empty list looks exactly like "no prior credit".

  • Why evals missed it: a fixture is always current, so this failure could not appear in any test
  • The fix was not a better model: the gate keeps a ledger of its commits, and stops when the balance exceeds listed credits
  • The thesis applies to the gate too: a belief can be well formed, freshly read and wrong
Logged in product/llm-failure-modes-log.md, with 29 other failures from this build
Tell it as a story, at the start of the decisions block. Also: one injection "catch" was really a parse failure, because failing closed hid a format bug behind a passing score. Reading the stored raw answers caught it.

12 · Guardrails

What it must not do

  • Never act on a failed read. Timeout, error or malformed reply gathers. Tested
  • Never move money without an act on that exact proposal, re-read before commit. Tested
  • Never settle a disagreement silently. The losing belief stays on the record. Built
  • Never apply a rule outside its scope or past its expiry. Tested
  • N3, kill switch: reversal rate 1 point above baseline over 7 days, or one reversal the gate should have caught, reverts that action type to approval and tells the VP and engineer within 15 minutes assumed Not built
19 tests, no model calls: ai-stack/test_warrant.py
22:00 to 23:30. Say the kill switch trigger as a number, and say plainly that it is designed, not built. Also not built: field-level access.

14 · Where this is weak

Three ways I could be wrong

  1. No customer has confirmed the problem. The evidence is public opinion classified by a model. No interviews, no log pull
  2. The results partly grade themselves. One model family wrote the system, the cases and the judge. My review of 15 records is the first outside check, by the author: 11 matched v3, 10 matched v4. Generated defects remove the model from the labels, but I still wrote the generator
  3. The live path is real but small. Scripted tickets, seeded accounts, one kind of write. A cited record shows it exists, not that the model read it, and P(safe) is not yet a calibrated probability
25:30 to 27:00. Never cut this slide. Volunteer it before Q&A. Point 2: on those 2 injection cases the gate was stricter than I was, held on the contract cap. Do not oversell it: 15 records, one reviewer. Ask: "What would change your view of the size of this problem by an order of magnitude?"

15 · Conclusions

What I would do next

  1. With half the time I would cut the boundary screen, shadow report and forum evidence, and keep the live path. It would cost the buyer story: a gate, not a product
  2. Before anyone builds this I would run five interviews and one log pull: how often do agents act on conflicting sources, and can finance tie it to leakage it already sees?
  3. In 90 days it worked if finance confirmed a meaningful share of held money as real leakage and the engineer widened autonomy on that evidence, with clean actions held at or below 5% assumed
Integration point: a stateful reasoning engine would replace belief.py, behind the same hard vetoes. Thank you · questions
27:00 to 30:00. End on the 90-day test, then stop talking. Q&A: "Where would this break first in production: volume, a bad input, or a system we do not control?" My answer after this build: a system we do not control. Buying conditions if asked: a log pull in dollars, a 30-day shadow pilot confirmed by finance, an independently measured over-hold rate, a built kill switch. The moat is data and workflow, not the model.
