Dyssonance enterprise AI challenge

Warrant

Support agents commit money on evidence that conflicts or is out of date, and nobody finds out until finance closes the month.

A gate between agents and the systems that move money: act, gather or hold.

5 Nov
0:00 to 1:00. "An agent can pass every check and still give away money it should not. I will show that happening to my own system." Say the plan: the problem, a live demo, the decisions, and where this is weak.

02 · The failure mode

Agents act on beliefs nobody checked

An agent commits an action on a belief that is contested or stale, and nothing records what it relied on.

  • Illustration: a retention agent credits an account that risk flagged an hour ago; a second agent then credits the same incident
  • Conflicts settle by mechanics: last write wins, or the most confident agent acts
  • Spawn lets whoever lands second reconcile, and keeps no record of the competing positions
  • The enterprise rate is unmeasured. That is the first thing to find out
Sources: Spawn CEO blog, 28 Oct 2026; MAST, arXiv 2503.13657 (research tasks, not enterprise)
1:00 to 2:00. Why this failure over the others: frequent, expensive, invisible until late, and a product can contain it. A better model does not fix it, because the evidence itself disagrees.

03 · The business problem

Who pays, and why they cannot see it

  • Support engineer: reversed credits and reopened tickets, never the conflict behind them
  • VP of Support: automation rises with no view of the clawbacks against it
  • Finance: knows the rough size at reconciliation, not which agent decision caused it
  • Why now: firms orchestrating multiple agents doubled in a quarter, 9% to 18% (KPMG)
  • Size: TAM $3.0B, SOM $25M ARR by year 3 assumed. Cost per incident is unmeasured
KPMG Q2 2026 AI Pulse, 204 US leaders at firms above $1B revenue. Finance point: one practitioner's view
2:00 to 3:30. Lead with throughput, not fear: more decisions act safely without a person. Sizing is bottom-up and assumed: 12,000 multi-agent enterprises at $100k to $500k a year; SOM is 100 accounts at $250k. Say assumed out loud.

07 · Target user and jobs to be done

The engineer who sets what agents may do alone

  • Engineer's job: when two of my agents reach different conclusions about a customer, I want their actions to match what is true across our systems, so I can let them do more without cleaning up after them
  • VP's job (buyer): when asked to automate more, I want to know it is not quietly creating rework and leakage, so I can keep growing the agent budget
  • Today: one decision, once, in a prompt or a permission toggle, never revisited with evidence
  • With this: per action type, from shadow evidence, widened or narrowed week by week
Evidence: public opinions classified by a model, including 70 Reddit snippets. No interviews yet
3:30 to 5:00. Too tight and the human queue grows, so why have agents. Too loose and reversals repeat. The analyst answers the question on the card; the engineer owns the boundary; the VP pays. Ask: "My assumption is the user already works in Slack. Correct me."

08 · Scope

Agents take money actions alone only when evidence agrees and is current

  • Not replacing the CRM vendor's agents, because this is separation of duties. A single-vendor shop is where we lose
  • Not a tracing product, because generic tracing is served. It writes only the decision record
  • Not changing models or prompts, because what it learns is stored as rules with an owner and an expiry
  • Assuming: agents' money actions are tool calls we can see, and sources expose read access with timestamps
5:00 to 7:00. The first non-goal has a losing case and I say it. Vertical is CRM service for now, because narrowing sharpened the design more than added research did. Ask: "I deliberately left severity disputes out of v1. Is that the right cut for your customers?"

11 · Where the AI was wrong

Every eval passed. Then the live agent double-credited a customer

Chargebee's credit list lagged its own write by over a minute. An empty list looks exactly like "no prior credit".

  • Why evals missed it: a fixture is always current, so this failure could not appear in any test
  • The fix was not a better model: the gate keeps a ledger of its commits, and stops when the balance exceeds listed credits
  • The thesis applies to the gate too: a belief can be well formed, freshly read and wrong
Logged in product/llm-failure-modes-log.md, with 27 other failures from this build
Tell it as a story, at the start of the decisions block. Also: one injection "catch" was really a parse failure, because failing closed hid a format bug behind a passing score. Reading the stored raw answers caught it.

12 · Guardrails

What it must not do

  • Never act on a failed read. Timeout, error or malformed reply gathers. Tested
  • Never move money without an act on that exact proposal, re-read before commit. Tested
  • Never settle a disagreement silently. The losing belief stays on the record. Built
  • Never apply a rule outside its scope or past its expiry. Tested
  • N3, kill switch: reversal rate 1 point above baseline over 7 days, or one reversal the gate should have caught, reverts that action type to approval and tells the VP and engineer within 15 minutes assumed Not built
19 tests, no model calls: ai-stack/test_warrant.py
22:00 to 24:00. Say the kill switch trigger as a number, and say plainly that it is designed, not built. Also not built: field-level access.

14 · Where this is weak

Three ways I could be wrong

  1. No customer has confirmed the problem. The evidence is public opinion classified by a model. No interviews, no log pull
  2. The results partly grade themselves. One model family wrote the system, the cases and the judge. My own review of 15 records is the first outside check
  3. The live path is real but small. Scripted tickets, seeded accounts, one kind of write. And every act still rests on a model finding nothing
25:30 to 27:00. Never cut this slide. Volunteer it before Q&A. Before presenting, update point 2 with what the human review found. Ask: "What would change your view of the size of this problem by an order of magnitude?"

15 · Conclusions

What I would do next

  1. With half the time I would cut the boundary screen, shadow report and forum evidence, and keep the live path. It would cost the buyer story: a gate, not a product
  2. Before anyone builds this I would run five interviews and one log pull: how often do agents act on conflicting sources, and can finance tie it to leakage it already sees?
  3. In 90 days it worked if finance confirmed a meaningful share of held money as real leakage and the engineer widened autonomy on that evidence, with clean actions held at or below 5% assumed
